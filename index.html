Introduction

Graph learning has been widely adopted in real-world applications to effectively capture complex structural dependencies and semantic information from graph-structured data. However, traditional methods, such as graph neural network (GNN)-based methods, face two fundamental limitations: they typically apply one-size-fits-all message-passing mechanisms to all nodes despite substantial structural and semantic heterogeneity, and they often require graph-specific training, resulting in limited transferability and generalization across different graphs and tasks.

In response, Agentic Graph Learning (AGL) has recently emerged as a promising new paradigm for graph learning. By leveraging the reasoning and planning capabilities of large language models, AGL formulates graph learning as a sequential decision-making process, where agents autonomously plan their interactions with graph environments and progressively acquire task-relevant structural and semantic evidence through graph-native tools.

In this tutorial, we aim to provide a comprehensive introduction to AGL, spanning its foundations and recent frontiers. We will cover: (1) the fundamental concepts, motivations, and key building blocks of agentic graph learning; (2) a systematic taxonomy of existing AGL methods, including single-agent graph reasoning, multi-agent collaboration, and their associated training strategies and applications; and (3) the key limitations, open challenges, and future research directions of AGL. Through this tutorial, we aim to provide a clear foundation for AGL and inspire future research and applications.

Presenters

Prof. Jianxin Li

Professor, Edith Cowan University

Prof. Jianxin Li is a Professor with extensive research experience in information systems, data science, artificial intelligence, and graph-related learning problems. He has published around 200 peer-reviewed research papers in leading international conferences and journals, including SIGMOD, PVLDB, ICDE, WWW, KDD, IEEE TKDE, Knowledge-Based Systems, and Information Systems. A number of his publications have been recognized as Highly Cited Papers or Hot Papers. He has secured more than ten externally funded research projects with a total value of approximately AUD 1.7 million, including Australian Research Council Discovery and Linkage Projects. He has more than 16 years of teaching experience across several Australian universities, covering undergraduate and postgraduate courses in data science, artificial intelligence, and business intelligence. He currently serves as Associate Editor for several international journals, including Information Systems, Knowledge-Based Systems, IEEE Signal Processing Letters, and World Wide Web. He has also served in major conference leadership roles, including Program Committee Chair or Steering Committee Member for ADMA 2019, APWeb-WAIM 2023, and numerous international workshops.

jianxin.li@ecu.edu.au

Dr. Liang Qu

Lecturer, Griffith University

Dr. Liang Qu is a Lecturer in the School of Information and Communication Technology at Griffith University. He received his PhD from the School of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at The University of Queensland in 2024 and subsequently worked as a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at Edith Cowan University in 2025. His research focuses on graph learning, agentic AI, and recommender systems. He has published more than 40 research papers in leading journals and conferences in artificial intelligence, data mining, and information retrieval, including IEEE TKDE, ACM TOIS, WWW, KDD, and SIGIR. His recent research explores agent-based learning and collaboration over graph-structured data, which is closely aligned with the central topic of this tutorial. He currently serves as an Associate Editor of Array and regularly reviews papers for major international journals and conferences.

liang.qu@griffith.edu.au
