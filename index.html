Introduction

Graphs are everywhere, from social networks and molecules to knowledge graphs, traffic systems and citation networks. Graph learning has evolved from shallow node embeddings to graph neural networks, graph transformers and, most recently, large language models (LLMs) for graphs. Yet most of these methods still read a graph once, through a fixed view: an LLM given a serialized graph cannot decide what to look at next, overflows its context on large graphs, and struggles with exact multi-hop reasoning.

Agentic Graph Learning (AGL) treats graph learning as an interactive process. An LLM agent plans its reasoning, calls graph tools (neighbour retrieval, text search, graph algorithms and code execution), observes the results, keeps a memory of the evidence, and decides when it has enough to answer. Multiple agents can further split the work, for example by node, by subgraph or by role.

This tutorial offers a systematic introduction to AGL: (1) the evolution of graph learning and the motivation for agents; (2) a unified framework and taxonomy of single-agent and multi-agent AGL methods, organised by planning, tools, memory, stopping and learning; (3) four open challenges, namely scalability and efficiency, reliability of the reasoning process, generalization and structure understanding, together with future directions; and (4) datasets, benchmarks and resources for getting started. We hope it lays a foundation and sparks new ideas for research on agentic graph learning.
